BEFORE
  pg_default_acl  postgres / public / functions
    {postgres=X/postgres, anon=X/postgres, authenticated=X/postgres, service_role=X/postgres}
  13 non-extension functions anon-executable:
    9 trigger functions (3 of them SECURITY DEFINER) + 4 helpers
  Every function created from then on would have arrived anon-executable.
  0067 is the proof: created 3 days earlier, landed with anon=X.

THE FIRST DRAFT WOULD NOT HAVE WORKED, AND THE HARNESS SAID SO
  It revoked `from anon, authenticated` and stopped. Rehearsal came back with
  audit_remarks_immutable STILL anon-executable:

    {=X/postgres, postgres=X/postgres, service_role=X/postgres}

  The leading `=X` with no grantee is PUBLIC, and anon is a member of PUBLIC.
  All thirteen carry BOTH a PUBLIC grant and a direct one.

  THIS IS THE 0067 DEFECT POINTING THE OTHER WAY. 0067 revoked PUBLIC and left
  the direct grant; the draft revoked the direct grant and left PUBLIC. Both
  halves must be named. Applied to production, the draft would have read like a
  fix and changed nothing.

  And the trigger question is answered by the suite, not by reasoning: dozens

THE SUITE

  The one that went green is "no trigger function is executable by anon or
  authenticated" -- a failure that had been red on main with no diagnosis until
  0067's own defect led to its cause.

A CORRECTION CARRIED INTO BOTH REGISTERS
  RC-042 and migrationGrants.test.ts said a `create or replace` of an unrevoked
  function would silently re-acquire the grant. That is WRONG: PostgreSQL
  preserves a function's ACL across CREATE OR REPLACE. The real exposure was
  new functions, and functions dropped and recreated. Still real -- 0067 was
  new -- but narrower than first written, and overstating a security finding is
  the easier mistake to leave standing.
